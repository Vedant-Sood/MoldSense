# Scientific basis and explicit heuristics

This is a screening instrument for environmental conditions and relative wall dielectric response. There is no validated mapping from its score to a frequentist probability of mold presence. The UI calls it an index; protocol field `mold_probability` is retained for interface compatibility and accompanied by `score_semantics=UNVALIDATED_SCREENING_INDEX`. LOW does not establish absence of mold, particularly with missing inputs or recent placement.

## Literature anchors

- EPA recommends keeping indoor RH below 60%, ideally 30–50%, and addressing wet materials within 24–48 hours. These are prevention/action guidelines, not mold-detection thresholds. [EPA, A Brief Guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).
- WHO emphasizes preventing/remediating persistent dampness and does not establish a quantitative health-based mold-contamination threshold. [WHO guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789289041683), [guideline conclusions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK143942/).
- Surface humidity and temperature, material sensitivity, and exposure duration matter. ASHRAE's 2016 addendum replaced a simple historical surface-RH criterion with an empirical mold-index approach. The sensitive-material initiation curve used here is RHcrit = −0.00267 T³ + 0.160 T² − 3.13 T + 100 below/equal 20°C, and 80% above 20°C. It is only consulted for a **measured** surface temperature in the growth range. [ASHRAE 160-2009 Addendum e, published 2016](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/160_2009_e_201601209.pdf).
- Building surfaces can have high RH despite moderate room RH. [ASHRAE fundamentals chapter](https://handbook.ashrae.org/Handbooks/F21/IP/f21_ch25/f21_ch25_ip.aspx). This firmware is **not** a complete VTT implementation or an ASHRAE 160 compliance calculation: it lacks material coefficients, actual surface temperature by default, moisture transport and validated growth/decline kinetics.
- BME AI-Studio exports application-specific trained configurations; raw gas resistance alone has no mold label. [Bosch training documentation](https://www.bosch-sensortec.com/software/bme/docs/process-steps/train-algorithms.html), [Bosch software and licensing](https://www.bosch-sensortec.com/en/software-tools/software/bme688-and-bme690-software).

## Transparent computation

All components below are fractions 0–1 internally. `clamp` bounds a finite value to that interval. Invalid/unavailable terms stay unavailable.

1. Air intensity = clamp((RH−60)/(90−60)), modified by a broad temperature suitability factor. The factor is 1 between 10–40°C, linearly decreases toward 0.2 at 0/50°C, and has a 0.2 floor outside; it never claims cold/dry conditions prove past mold absent. The 90% ramp endpoint and this temperature factor are **engineering heuristics**, not literature-fitted growth rates.
2. If a surface probe is supplied, compute surface RH by equal vapor pressure using the Magnus saturation-vapor approximation. Environmental intensity is the maximum of air intensity and excess above the sensitive-material RHcrit curve. Without it, no air-temperature-as-wall-temperature substitution is made.
3. Dew point uses Magnus constants 17.625 and 243.04°C. Near-condensation intensity = clamp((3−(Tsurface−Tdew))/3). The 3°C margin is an engineering early-warning margin; estimated condensation occurs at/below dew point. No actual wall condensation term is available in the supplied hardware.
4. Wall index = 100 × clamp((wetDirection × (filtered−baseline)−deadband)/(span−deadband)). Deadband is max(3% of baseline, 3 × baseline SD); span is 30% of baseline with a 2-unit denominator floor. Touch wetDirection = −1. This maps a local relative response to an instrument scale, **not moisture content percent**. The 3%, 30%, 2-unit floor and 25-index alert all require probe/material characterization.
5. Exposure doses integrate intensity over **observed valid time**. Air and wall horizons are 24 h; measured condensation horizon 6 h. With zero intensity a linear 48 h screening-memory decay applies. Missing data freezes accumulated dose and breaks continuity. No gap over 5 s is integrated. Counters cap at 720 observed hours. These durations are engineering choices inspired by sustained-dampness guidance, not validated time-to-growth predictions.
6. Environmental and condensation components = 20% immediate intensity + 80% normalized dose. Wall component = 65% immediate relative response + 35% dose, so an abnormal wet wall becomes actionable quickly while persistence continues increasing the score.
7. Classifier evidence is available only with an explicitly validated, accepted configuration, correct class mapping, finite 0–1 class outputs summing near 1, a fresh complete class vector and BSEC gas accuracy. IAQ/eCO2/bVOC and raw gas resistance are **not** translated into mold probability.
8. Fuse in fixed order environment, wall, condensation, VOC: `score = 100 × (1 − product(1 − weight[i] × component[i]))`. Weights are 0.50, 0.70, 0.35, 0.65. They are adjustable **uncalibrated engineering weights**; the noisy-OR form does not establish independence or a Bayesian posterior. Missing terms do not increase the remaining weights. A negative/low VOC class never cancels moisture evidence.
9. Contribution `i = 100 × residual_before_i × weight[i] × component[i]`. Contributions sum exactly to the index; their allocation depends on the documented fixed order. They are debugging attributions, not causal explanations.
10. Confidence is an availability/quality score: valid air 30 points, baseline-qualified wall up to 40, measured surface 10, validated VOC up to 20. Touch wall quality reserves 10% because connectivity/contact cannot be proven. It is not a statistical confidence interval. With no surface/model, even ideal air+touch baseline yields about 66%. Below 20 points the risk level is UNKNOWN.

Risk bands: LOW <25; ELEVATED 25–<50; HIGH ≥50; CRITICAL requires index ≥75 **and six continuous observed hours at HIGH or above**. These thresholds are configurable response categories, not externally validated mold probabilities. Single humidity spikes cannot produce CRITICAL. A continuously observed very wet wall and 95% RH can reach 85 after the dose saturates without any VOC model; this is evidence of strongly favorable conditions, not proof of mold.

## Filtering and calibration decisions

Seven-reading median, 0.18 EMA, and a 12% inner-range stability check reject spikes without permanently rejecting a true step. Reconnection clears the EMA. Baseline sampling: 60 s at 500 ms, ≥80% usable samples; reject beyond max(2 units, 3 × 1.4826 × MAD); accepted CV ≤3%, half-window drift ≤3%, RH range ≤5 percentage points, temperature range ≤2°C. All are instrument repeatability requirements, not material moisture constants. Baseline confidence falls with sample loss and variation.

## Research validity

Use independently verified ground truth, blinded unknowns, dry and wet controls, unrelated VOC sources, multiple independent walls/materials/days, and fixed acquisition geometry. Separate train/test by physical sample and day to avoid leakage. Thousands of adjacent readings are not thousands of independent replicates. Desktop Pearson/Spearman are descriptive; no causal inference or per-reading confidence interval is claimed. Effect sizes or confidence intervals should use independent session/sample replicates with the appropriate study design. Keep heater profile, step, sensor and model version comparable in gas analyses.
